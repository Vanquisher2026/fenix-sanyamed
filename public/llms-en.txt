# TAIJI Sanatorium (fenix-sanyamed)
> A medical centre of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) in the city of Sanya, Hainan Island, China. Operating since 2007. Holds a PRC state licence to practise traditional Chinese medicine. Four locations across the bays of Dadonghai, Yalong Bay and Sanya Bay. A team of physicians with more than 40 years of experience each. An English-speaking medical coordinator is included in the programme price, airport transfer is free, and the first consultation with pulse diagnosis is free. Russian citizens may enter Hainan visa-free for up to 30 days.

## What this site is

A medical centre in Sanya providing traditional Chinese medicine services to international patients. The core audience is visitors from Europe, North America, Australia and the wider world who want a course of TCM in China and to combine it with recovery on a tropical island.

The programme is selected by the physician individually after pulse diagnosis. Patients continue any prescribed medication: TCM is a complement to primary medical care, not a replacement for it.

- Acupuncture (针灸)

## Frequently asked questions (short answers)

Q: How much does treatment in China cost?
A: One session 150–450 CNY, a 7-day programme from 8,000 CNY, a 14-day programme from 15,000 CNY, excluding accommodation and flights.

Q: Do I need a visa for Hainan?
A: Not for Russian citizens (visa-free for up to 30 days). Most other nationalities either do not need one for a short stay or can obtain a visa quickly in advance. Requirements depend on passport — ask with the passport details.

Q: How long does a treatment course take?
A: Typically 7–14 days; the physician sets the duration after the consultation.

Q: Is there English-speaking staff?
A: Yes. An English-speaking coordinator accompanies every consultation and is included in the programme price. A Russian-speaking medical translator is also available on site.

Q: Is acupuncture safe?
A: Single-use sterile needles are used. Before the course the physician collects the history and checks contraindications.

Q: Does TCM replace conventional medicine?
A: No. TCM is a complement to primary medical care, not a replacement. Patients continue any prescribed medication.

Q: Is a result guaranteed?
A: No. The response is individual: for some patients the effect is pronounced, for others moderate, and for some there is little change.

Q: How does Hainan differ from the sanatoriums of Wudalianchi or Anshan?
A: Hainan is a tropical island (January around +21 °C) with visa-free entry and direct flights. The north-eastern Chinese resorts rely on mineral waters and mud, but sit in a sharply continental climate with winter frosts to −25 °C.

Q: Can I pay with a foreign card?
A: Cash in CNY, WeChat Pay, Alipay and UnionPay are accepted. International cards work at some, but not all, providers — bring a backup method.

Q: Should I bring my MRI images?
A: Yes, if you have them — the physician reviews them before the programme begins.
